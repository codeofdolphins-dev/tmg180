TMG180 — Relational Evidence Notes
Participant-Owned Longitudinal Support Mapping
Purpose of These Notes
These notes are not about judging you.
They are not about trying to “catch you out,” prove failure, or reduce your life to symptoms.
They exist to help build a clearer picture over time of:
what everyday life actually feels like
what support helps
what becomes harder during stress, illness, overload, burnout, or change
how your capacity fluctuates
what recovery costs look like
and what support makes participation possible
The NDIS increasingly expects participants to understand and explain their own functional impacts over time.
For many people, especially people with psychosocial disability, neurodivergence, trauma histories, chronic illness, pain, fatigue, or fluctuating capacity, this can be very difficult to explain from memory alone.
These notes help build that picture gradually.
Nothing here is about perfection.
A harder day does not mean failure.
Needing support does not mean you are doing something wrong.
Improvement with support does not mean support is no longer needed.
The goal is not to remove your voice.
The goal is to help organise patterns over time so your experience becomes easier to understand, explain, and evidence when needed.

How These Notes Work
Workers
Workers complete a short structured support note after a session.
The purpose is not to write long reports.
The purpose is to capture:
what support was needed
what affected the participant that day
what support looked like
what changed as a result
and whether anything was different from usual patterns
Notes should stay:
respectful
neutral
non-shaming
practical
and focused on impact first
Avoid:
pathologising language
assumptions
judgment
unnecessary clinical wording
or writing “about” the participant rather than with respect to their experience

Participants
Participants may also complete check-ins or reflections if they wish.
This is optional.
You do not need to explain yourself perfectly.
You are not expected to use professional language.
Your own words matter.
Over time, these notes can help identify:
recurring patterns
triggers
recovery costs
support effectiveness
participation changes
and what helps maintain stability

Important Principles
Capacity Can Fluctuate
Some people have stable support needs.
Others experience significant fluctuation depending on:
stress
burnout
sensory load
pain
fatigue
trauma responses
health
environment
or life events
Fluctuation is real.
A participant may:
appear capable one day
and need significantly more support another day.
This does not automatically mean regression or lack of effort.

Support Changes Outcomes
If participation improves with support, this reflects support working.
It does not automatically mean impairment has disappeared.
Many participants can participate more consistently because:
support reduced recovery cost
tasks were scaffolded
overwhelm was reduced
routines were stabilised
or safety/support needs were met
These notes help show that relationship over time.

Tone Expectations for All Notes
Use:
everyday language
impact-first descriptions
relational wording
participant dignity
practical observations
short structured responses
Avoid:
diagnostic speculation
labelling
exaggerated language
emotionally loaded wording
deficit-only framing
or language that removes participant autonomy
Instead of:
“participant was non-compliant”
Use:
“participant appeared overwhelmed and unable to continue the task today”
Instead of:
“participant failed to regulate emotions”
Use:
“participant became distressed during increased stress and required reduced demands and calm support”

What These Notes Build Over Time
When completed consistently, these notes help create:
longitudinal evidence
functional pattern tracking
support frequency mapping
recovery cost visibility
fluctuation evidence
sustainability tracking
participation outcomes
support effectiveness evidence
and participant-owned summaries
This means reassessments do not rely only on memory.
The system gradually builds a clearer picture over time.

Relational Structure Guidance for Developers
The structure itself should remain:
short
repeatable
structured
low cognitive load
mobile-friendly
and easy to complete consistently
Each note block should continue mapping:
Functional impact
Support required
Support delivered
Outcome/change
Baseline comparison
Recovery/sustainability indicators
Participant voice
This architecture should not be altered because it is what allows:
longitudinal comparison
pattern detection
structured summarisation
fluctuation mapping
and AI-supported evidence translation
The relational language exists on top of the structure — not instead of it.
