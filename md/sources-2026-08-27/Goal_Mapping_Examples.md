TMG Section 1: Mapping Support to
Goals and Legislation

This section explains how to confidently and ethically connect participant supports to their
stated goals in a way that aligns with NDIS legislative requirements, particularly Section 34
of the NDIS Act. It is written to support independent workers, auditors, and participants
using the TMG Framework. This process reflects relational discipline, ensures legal
defensibility, and promotes participant-centred practice.

1. Relational Discipline Meets 'Reasonable and Necessary'

Relational Discipline is the foundation of this framework. It recognises that connection,
trust, and emotional safety are not just desirable - they are essential to support
effectiveness. However, under the NDIS, supports must also be 'reasonable and necessary'
under Section 34 of the Act. This means that supports must:
∑ Be related to the participant's disability
∑ Not be more appropriately funded by another service
∑ Represent value for money
∑ Be likely to benefit the participant
∑ Support the pursuit of goals

Workers often worry that relational supports - such as providing stability, encouragement,
or helping a participant manage emotional dysregulation - may not `count'. But these are
often *the most necessary* for a person to access other supports or build capacity.
Emotional regulation, social engagement, and psychological safety are functional capacities.

2. Functional Capacity Domains

NDIS recognises six functional capacity domains. Any support linked to these areas, when
clearly connected to the participant's disability and goals, is valid. They are:
∑ Mobility
∑ Communication
∑ Social interaction
∑ Learning
∑ Self-care
∑ Self-management
Relational supports (like helping someone manage anxiety when leaving the house) directly
impact multiple domains, especially social interaction and self-management.

3. Intake  Goal  Support Examples

∑ Example 1:

Goal: "Improve my mental and physical health."
Support: Weekly walks with a support worker who understands trauma-informed care.
Justification: Linked to mobility, self-care, and social interaction. May also include meal prep
or healthy shopping if nutrition is affected by medication or mental health.
Evidence Phrase: "Support builds participant's capacity to improve wellbeing through
structured, safe engagement that promotes emotional regulation and healthy routines."

∑ Example 2:

Goal: "Have a sense of purpose and connection."
Support: Regular home visits with a relationally trained worker to explore strengths and
support the creation of a blog or small project.
Justification: Linked to social interaction, learning, and self-management. May reduce
isolation and build confidence to access the community.
Evidence Phrase: "Support enables participant to explore purposeful activity aligned with
their goals, and reduces functional impact of isolation or trauma symptoms."

4. Legal Notes for Workers

NDIS does not require full clinical justification in notes, but does require that supports are
clearly linked to functional impact and goals. Always:
∑ Refer to participant goals
∑ Use the intake form as a dynamic document
∑ Describe outcomes in terms of functional benefit
∑ Use respectful, strength-based language

Additional Examples to Expand Understanding

Example 2:
Goal: "Increase social and community participation."
Support: Participant engages in a weekly online peer group facilitated by a worker using
trauma-informed relational practices.
Justification: Linked to communication and social interaction domains. Supports connection
and reduces social isolation, especially where in-person participation is not yet accessible.
Evidence Phrase: "Digital social support enables progressive engagement with community,
reducing psychological barriers and fostering inclusion."

Example 3:
Goal: "Build my capacity to start a small business."
Support: Worker assists participant to develop a content calendar, build routines, and
explore basic digital skills necessary for a micro-enterprise.
Justification: Linked to learning, communication, and self-management domains. Addresses
purpose, economic participation, and self-direction.
Evidence Phrase: "Support focuses on capacity building through structured, goal-oriented
engagement aligned with the participant's long-term aspirations."

Example 4:
Goal: "Improve day-to-day functioning and independence."
Support: Support worker assists with domestic activities like cleaning and meal prep to
reduce overwhelm and build routines.
Justification: Linked to self-care and self-management. Ensures energy and focus are
available for therapy, appointments, and goal setting.
Evidence Phrase: "Domestic assistance ensures basic needs are met and participant can
actively engage with broader capacity-building activities."
Example 5:
Goal: "Establish a routine and manage emotional regulation."

Support: Relational worker supports participant in developing and maintaining a weekly
structure, including mindfulness or check-in sessions.

Justification: Directly related to social interaction and self-management. Foundational for
engaging with other supports safely.

Evidence Phrase: "Structured emotional support reduces behavioural volatility and enables
continuity in therapeutic and capacity-building goals."
