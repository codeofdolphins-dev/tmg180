	| 1. Executive Summary
TMG180 was developed because a significant gap exists between the human-centred intent of the NDIS and the way support is often interpreted, documented and delivered in practice. The NDIS legislative framework contains strong principles of dignity, autonomy, choice and control, social and economic participation, reasonable-risk decision-making, supported decision-making, individualised planning, safeguarding and participant-directed supports. However, these principles can become thin or invisible when they are reduced to forms, provider routines, rigid interpretation, generic compliance documents or unsupported worker practice.
TMG180 responds to that gap by creating a relationally governed digital support ecosystem. It is not designed as a traditional NDIS provider, an employer, a clinical service, a plan manager, a support coordinator, a substitute decision-maker, a marketplace that controls service delivery, or a provider-led matching system. Its purpose is to provide governance infrastructure, relational practice standards, documentation tools, participant-owned evidence pathways, worker-facing guidance, platform boundaries and AI-assisted workflow support that make human-centred practice more visible, explainable, consistent and auditable.
The central governance thesis of TMG180 is that human-centred practice is not proven by values statements alone. It must be visible in how participants understand options, choose supports and workers, direct routines, raise concerns, document preferences, review arrangements, preserve autonomy, and build longitudinal evidence of support needs and outcomes over time.
CENTRAL GOVERNANCE POSITION
TMG180 was developed to bridge the space between legal principle and lived practice: legislation says choice, control, dignity, autonomy, participation and safeguarding matter; TMG180 asks what systems, documents, workflows and relational standards are needed so those principles are visible in everyday support.
	| 2. Why TMG180 Was Developed
2.1 The identified sector gap
The development of TMG180 was driven by the observation that many participants and independent workers were operating inside systems that had become increasingly fragmented, administratively heavy and difficult to interpret. Some people genuinely cared and wanted to help, but still interpreted support through their own assumptions about what a participant should need, how independence should look, or what a provider-defined support pathway should be.
This is a different problem from simple non-compliance. A service can be caring, organised and documented while still being provider-led. A worker can be kind while still unconsciously substituting their own view of what should happen. A provider can say it supports choice while only offering a narrow menu of options. A support plan can appear tidy while the participant’s real voice, preferences and reasoning are thin or absent.
TMG180 was developed to make the missing human layer operational: not just stating that participants have choice and control, but showing how choice and control appears in worker fit, timing, support style, documentation, participant review, relational safety and longitudinal evidence.
2.2 The gap between legislation and everyday interpretation
The NDIS Act and related governance frameworks contain strong human-centred principles, but those principles need translation into daily practice. TMG180 was developed because legal concepts such as choice and control, reasonable and necessary supports, supported decision-making, individualised planning, dignity of risk and social/economic participation can be misunderstood or narrowed when they pass through provider routines, audit checklists, service catalogues, workforce shortages or individual interpretation.
The issue is not only whether paperwork exists. The issue is whether an independent reviewer can follow the reasoning from the participant’s own life and support needs through to the support delivered, evidence recorded and decisions reviewed.
2.3 The worker sustainability gap
TMG180 also recognises that independent workers are often expected to carry complex relational, documentation, safeguarding and administrative responsibilities without adequate practical infrastructure. Good workers can burn out when systems are fragmented, language is unclear, governance feels punitive, and documentation requirements are disconnected from the actual human work being performed.
The platform therefore supports worker sustainability without becoming an employer or directing service delivery. It provides operational tools, practice standards, templates, guidance and clarity so workers can operate independently with stronger governance support.
2.4 The participant evidence gap
Participants with psychosocial disability, fluctuating capacity, chronic illness, trauma histories, executive dysfunction or complex life circumstances are often poorly represented through isolated assessments or task-based notes. Their support needs may be contextual, variable and cumulative. TMG180 was developed to support longitudinal evidence that shows patterns over time, not just isolated events.
This is why the ecosystem includes Personal Profiles, Daily Support Evidence Logs, Monthly Snapshot Summaries, participant check-ins, goal-linking support, and AI-assisted translation tools. These are not surveillance tools. They are designed to reduce repeated retelling, preserve participant voice, and make support needs clearer over time.
3. Legislative and Governance Foundations
TMG180 is grounded in a layered governance interpretation of the NDIS and related frameworks. The architecture uses legislation and governance principles as anchors, then translates them into platform boundaries, practice standards, evidence pathways and worker/participant-facing tools.
Governance foundation
	| TMG180 interpretation
	| NDIS Act s 3 – Objects
	| The ecosystem is designed around independence, social and economic participation, participant goals, choice and control, and support that helps people live ordinary, meaningful lives.
	| NDIS Act s 4 – General principles
	| The platform treats participants as people with autonomy, identity, reasonable-risk choices, relationships and equal partnership in decisions affecting their lives.
	| NDIS Act s 5 – Acts and things done on behalf of participants
	| TMG180 emphasises supported decision-making and participant involvement wherever another person assists with decisions or support processes.
	| NDIS Act s 7 – Communication
	| Platform guidance and documents must use accessible, understandable language rather than hidden professional or provider-only interpretation.
	| NDIS Act s 17A – Principles relating to people with disability
	| Capacity should be presumed where reasonable and supports should maximise choice and control, not replace decision-making because support takes time.
	| NDIS Act s 31 – Plan preparation and funding management
	| Planning and support use should remain individualised, participant-directed, flexible and underpinned by control over the participant’s own life.
	| NDIS Act s 34 – Reasonable and necessary supports
	| Support interpretation must link impairment-related support need, goals, participation, effectiveness, value for money, informal supports and NDIS support status.
	| NDIS Code of Conduct
	| Worker and platform conduct must support dignity, safety, integrity, transparency, privacy, and respect for self-determination.
	| NDIS Practice Standards
	| Where relevant, the platform design reflects informed choice and control, responsive support, participant involvement, complaints, incidents and governance clarity.
	| Privacy Act / APPs / NDB
	| The platform uses data minimisation, privacy by design and separation between platform data and worker-controlled/private records.
	| WHS and psychosocial risk
	| Worker sustainability, emotional safety, digital safety, burnout prevention and psychologically safer workflows are treated as governance design issues.

5. TMG180 Foundational Precepts
The Foundational Precepts establish the philosophical and governance basis for the ecosystem. They describe TMG180 as a relationally governed digital support ecosystem designed to support participant-centred, longitudinal and operationally sustainable support environments.
The precepts recognise that the real foundation of the NDIS is the relationship between the people providing support and the people receiving support. They also recognise that people cannot be reduced to diagnoses, behavioural labels, funding categories or compliance systems alone.
Foundational precept
	| Governance reason
	| Ecosystem response
	| People are whole human beings
	| Support systems can reduce people to diagnosis, risk, behaviour or funding categories.
	| Personal Profile, relational language standards, participant voice prompts, support interpretation library.
	| Governance and humanity must coexist
	| Compliance without human-centred interpretation can become controlling or hollow.
	| Relational governance standards, practice guidance, governance-controlled rationale documents.
	| Autonomy and safeguarding must be held together
	| Safety can be used to remove ordinary choice; autonomy without support can leave people exposed.
	| Choice/control framework, proportional risk language, incident/complaints pathways, worker fit safeguards.
	| Worker independence needs operational support
	| Independent workers remain self-employed but need clear systems to operate safely and sustainably.
	| Worker portal, onboarding, document templates, credential checks, invoicing/evidence tools.
	| Psychosocial complexity requires context
	| Fluctuating support needs are poorly represented in isolated assessments or generic case notes.
	| Longitudinal evidence model: baseline profile, daily logs, participant check-ins and monthly snapshots.
	| Digital infrastructure should serve human support
	| Technology can fragment, surveil or over-automate human relationships if not governed.
	| AI rules, platform boundaries, privacy architecture, human oversight and data minimisation.
	| WHY THIS MATTERS
The Foundational Precepts explain why TMG180 was not built as a simple directory, a provider model or a compliance package. It was built as relational infrastructure: a way to hold governance, participant autonomy, worker sustainability and human-centred support together.
	| 6. Relational Discipline Framework
Relational Discipline is the practice philosophy that gives TMG180 its human-centred governance shape. It is values-based, human-centred and grounded in trust, respect and accountability. It treats connection, mutuality and transparency as foundational rather than optional.
Relational Discipline is not permissiveness and not rigid professionalism. It is the discipline of using warmth, boundaries, communication, self-awareness, consistency and ethical judgement to support another person well.
Relational discipline element
	| Why it was needed
	| Governance expression
	| Warmth with boundaries
	| Participants often need relational safety, but boundary confusion can create harm.
	| Relational Practice Standards, ethical fit guidance, boundary and attachment standards.
	| Non-shaming accountability
	| Punitive systems cause silence, defensiveness and delayed disclosure.
	| Feedback/complaints framework, relational repair language, concern-before-complaint pathways.
	| Power awareness
	| Support relationships involve access, influence, continuity and the ability to remain or withdraw.
	| Worker responsibility guidance, participant voice, platform boundaries and safeguarding controls.
	| Contextual interpretation
	| Behaviour, distress and participation can be misunderstood when context is ignored.
	| Support interpretation manual, trauma-aware practice standards, longitudinal evidence.
	| Ethical fit
	| A worker may be competent and still not the right relational fit for a particular support context.
	| Worker profiles, participant choice tools, fit/reflection standards and transition pathways.
	| 7. Choice and Control in Practice
TMG180 treats choice and control as a practical governance issue, not as an abstract value. Choice and control means the participant receives information they can understand, is supported to express their will and preferences, has real options, meaningfully influences decisions that affect them, and can review or change those decisions within lawful and safe limits.
The Choice and Control framework also protects against provider-led decision-making being disguised as support. A provider or worker may recommend, explain, assess competence, manage availability and decline unsafe or out-of-scope requests. The concern arises when provider preference silently substitutes for participant choice, or where risk/compliance language ends the discussion rather than supporting an informed decision.
Choice/control domain
	| TMG180 design response
	| Choosing supports
	| Plain-language support interpretation, goal-linking prompts and worker guidance that separates participant goals from provider programs.
	| Choosing workers / relational fit
	| Directory/profile tools designed around participant preference, communication style, fit and optional relational indicators, without platform-controlled matching.
	| Timing and routines
	| Documentation prompts that recognise support should fit the participant’s ordinary life where possible, not only provider roster convenience.
	| Accessible information
	| Website layers, library resources, plain-language documents, AI output modes and participant-facing explanations.
	| Safe complaints and feedback
	| Relational concern pathways, no-retaliation principles, participant-owned records and external complaint visibility.
	| Evidence of choice/control
	| Case-note and snapshot structures showing what the participant wanted, what was explained, what they chose and how support changed.
	| 8. Plans, Goals, Support Needs and Funded Supports
A major reason TMG180 needed an interpretation layer is that plan language is often misunderstood. Goals, support needs, funded supports and the plan itself are connected, but they are not interchangeable.
Concept
	| Meaning
	| Why the distinction matters
	| Goals
	| What the participant wants to move toward, maintain, change, experience or achieve.
	| Goals provide direction but are not itemised shopping lists or provider programs.
	| Support needs
	| The assistance required because an impairment affects everyday functioning.
	| Support needs are not simply diagnosis; they describe what is difficult, when, how often, what happens without assistance and what support helps.
	| Funded supports
	| The actual assistance, service, item or equipment the NDIS agrees to fund.
	| Funding must meet statutory criteria; not every meaningful activity is funded, but disability-related assistance enabling participation may be.
	| The plan
	| The formal funding framework recording goals, approved funding, management and review arrangements.
	| A plan is a funding map, not a life prescription, weekly timetable or provider-controlled program.
	| TMG180 tools maintain this distinction through the Personal Profile, Goal Link Helper, support interpretation guidance, Daily Support Evidence Logs and Monthly Snapshot Summaries. This helps prevent workers, providers or AI systems from collapsing a participant’s life into a diagnosis, a provider category or a narrow activity list.

	| 10. Platform Boundary Statement
Platform boundaries are central to TMG180’s legal and governance defensibility. The platform must be clear about what it provides and what it does not provide.
TMG180 does
	| TMG180 does not
	| Provides governance infrastructure, templates, platform-level policies, evidence tools and learning resources.
	| Does not control service delivery, supervise workers or manage participant supports.
	| Supports participant choice through information, profiles, optional preference indicators and transparent worker information.
	| Does not assign workers, operate a provider roster, or act as a provider-led matching service.
	| Maintains platform-level incident/complaint and digital safety pathways.
	| Does not adjudicate all disputes between participants and workers or replace external complaint bodies.
	| Verifies worker eligibility documents and maintains platform access conditions.
	| Does not become the worker’s employer or assume responsibility for every aspect of the worker’s business.
	| Provides AI-assisted drafting and interpretation tools within governed limits.
	| Does not use AI to assess funding, diagnose, make decisions, override participant voice or determine legal rights.
	| Supports documentation and longitudinal evidence structures.
	| Does not own or control all worker service records or replace clinical/allied health assessment.
	| BOUNDARY PRINCIPLE
TMG180 is governance-supported operational infrastructure, not provider-controlled service delivery. This distinction must be preserved across documents, website language, platform design, contracts, AI outputs and audit materials.
	| 11. AI Governance Register
AI was included because the system needed a way to reduce administrative burden, support consistent interpretation, help workers write clearer evidence and preserve longitudinal context. However, AI also creates governance risks if it flattens complexity, invents conclusions, over-automates judgement or replaces participant voice.
AI governance area
	| Control / rationale
	| Role of AI
	| Assistive drafting, summarisation, translation and pattern-support only.
	| Human oversight
	| Workers and participants remain responsible for review, context and final meaning.
	| No autonomous decisions
	| AI must not decide funding, assess capacity, diagnose, escalate administratively, rank participants or determine legal outcomes.
	| Participant voice
	| AI must preserve participant words and distinguish participant voice from worker observation and system interpretation.
	| Relational language
	| AI must avoid punitive, pathologising, provider-led or compliance-heavy language unless legally necessary.
	| Privacy
	| AI must operate within approved platform governance and avoid public AI exposure of identifiable participant information.
	| Traceability
	| Outputs should be reviewable, explainable and linked to source fields or approved knowledge base material where possible.
	| Drift control
	| Terminology registries, banned terms, output mode controls and review cycles are required.

	| 19. Conclusion
TMG180 exists because the principles of the NDIS and human-centred support need to be translated into systems people can actually use. The platform is not an attempt to replace providers, workers, participants, advocates, clinicians, the NDIA or regulators. It is an attempt to provide a governed middle ground where participant autonomy, worker sustainability, safeguarding, documentation, AI assistance and digital infrastructure can operate together without losing the human purpose of support.
The governance architecture is intentionally detailed because human-centred practice is often the part that is least visible in ordinary audits. TMG180 therefore documents not only what the platform does, but why it exists, what risks it responds to, how it interprets key NDIS principles, and how each component supports a traceable pathway from law and values into everyday practice.
FINAL GOVERNANCE STATEMENT
TMG180 is designed to make human-centred support operational, visible and reviewable. Its purpose is to preserve dignity, autonomy, relational safety, worker sustainability and governance integrity together.
