MODULE 1
Why TMG180 Exists
5 minute read  •  Foundation  •  TMG180 Learning Hub
	| 


	| Most support systems were not built for people like your participants. They were built for people who can describe their needs in clinical language, attend appointments consistently, and fit into standard categories. TMG180 was built for everyone else.
	| 

The problem this platform was built to solve

If you have been working in the NDIS for any length of time, you will have noticed something. The system talks about choice and control but the way it actually works often makes both very hard.

Participants who most need support are often the ones least able to prove they need it. At plan reassessment they are asked to explain two years of lived experience from memory. Workers write notes in clinical language that does not reflect what actually happened. Providers manage compliance but lose sight of people.

And independent workers — people who genuinely want to do good work — often burn out because they are carrying all of this without any infrastructure to support them.

TMG180 was built by someone who experienced this from the inside. Not as a provider. Not as a clinician. As a participant. Someone who spent years trying to explain her own support needs in a language the system could understand, while the system kept missing what actually mattered.


	| What she built is the infrastructure she wished had existed when she needed it.
	| 

What TMG180 actually is

TMG180 is not a rostering system. It is not a compliance checklist. It is not a marketplace.

It is a participant-owned evidence ecosystem. It builds a longitudinal record of a participant's support over time — from their own words, from structured worker observations, and from the patterns that emerge across both.

That record belongs to the participant. Not the provider. Not TMG180. The participant decides what is shared, with whom, and when.

When reassessment comes — which is when participants most need evidence — they do not have to remember everything from scratch. The evidence is already there. Built gradually. Verified by them. In their own language.


Why the relational language matters

Every word on this platform was chosen deliberately. The way the intake form is worded. The way the daily note templates are structured. The way the monthly summary describes progress.

This is not branding. It is not tone of voice.

The language is the architecture. If the platform used clinical or shaming language, participants would stop engaging honestly. If they stop engaging honestly, the evidence becomes inaccurate. If the evidence becomes inaccurate, the whole longitudinal model fails.

The relational language is what makes the evidence trustworthy. That is why it is non-negotiable.

MODULE 2
The Relational Framework
5 minute read  •  Core practice  •  TMG180 Learning Hub
	| 


	| Relational practice and governance accountability are not opposites. TMG180 was built to prove they can coexist — and to show what that actually looks like in practice.
	| 

What the relational framework is

The relational framework is the set of principles that governs how every interaction on this platform works — between you and participants, between workers and the platform, and between the platform and the outside world.

It has eight core principles. They are not aspirations. They are operational standards. Every screen, every template, every piece of language on this platform was built around them.


1. Autonomy without micromanagement
You are an independent worker. TMG180 does not supervise your practice or direct how you deliver support. You bring your own professional judgement to every session. The platform gives you infrastructure — not instructions.

2. Relational discipline
Warmth is not unprofessional. Connection, humour, and genuine care are part of good support. Clear role boundaries protect that warmth — they do not replace it. You may share aspects of your own life in an everyday, natural way. What you must not do is process your emotional struggles with participants, lean on them for support, or expect them to carry your feelings. That boundary exists to protect them.

3. Non-shaming language
A harder day is information. Not failure. A cancelled session is not non-compliance. A participant who is struggling is not going backwards. In every note you write, every observation you make, every conversation you have — language is neutral, specific, and dignity-preserving.

4. Participant as expert
Participants are experts in their own experience. You observe and support. You do not define their needs for them, override their stated preferences, or interpret their behaviour through a clinical lens without consent.

5. Non-linear functioning
Capacity fluctuates. This is especially true for people with psychosocial disability, trauma histories, chronic illness, or neurological differences. A harder week after a good week is not regression. It is how these conditions work. You never frame fluctuation as failure.

6. Confidentiality and consent
Your private narrative notes stay with you. Only structured observations flow into the participant's record — and only with their consent. Nothing is shared without the participant's knowledge. They can see everything written about them at any time.

7. Psychological safety first
If a participant is distressed, overwhelmed, or dysregulated — task completion is secondary. Regulation, safety, and connection come first. You are not expected to manage clinical risk alone. You are expected to recognise when a situation needs escalation and to follow the platform's incident pathway.

8. Dignity in documentation
Notes describe what happened and what the impact was. Not interpretations of character, motivation, or mental state. You write what you observed — not what you assumed. "The participant indicated they were not ready to begin today" is a note. "The participant was being difficult" is not.


Why governance and relational practice are the same thing on this platform

Many workers have experienced providers where compliance and care felt like opposites. Where writing a thorough, honest note felt risky. Where the system rewarded brevity over accuracy.

On TMG180 they are the same thing. A thorough, honest, dignity-preserving note is the most compliant note you can write. A relational interaction is the most defensible interaction you can have. The framework does not ask you to choose between good practice and safe practice. It asks you to understand that they are the same.

MODULE 3
Understanding Participants on This Platform
5 minute read  •  Practice knowledge  •  TMG180 Learning Hub
	| 


	| Most participants on TMG180 have spent years trying to explain their experience to systems that were not built to understand them. Your job is not to assess them. Your job is to support them — and to document what you see, honestly and consistently, over time.
	| 

Who you will be supporting

TMG180 was designed primarily for participants with psychosocial disability. This includes people living with mental health conditions, trauma histories, PTSD, ADHD, autism, chronic illness, chronic pain, acquired brain injury, and complex combinations of these.

Many of these participants appear high-functioning. They may be articulate, intelligent, and capable in many areas. And they may simultaneously struggle significantly with things that seem simple — starting tasks, leaving the house, maintaining routines, managing paperwork, or sustaining energy across a day.

This is the hidden nature of psychosocial disability. And it is one of the main reasons these participants have often been poorly served by standard NDIS systems that look for visible, consistent, easily measured impairment.


Fluctuating capacity — what it actually means

Fluctuating capacity means a participant's ability to function changes — sometimes dramatically — depending on stress, health, sleep, environment, emotional load, and other factors that are often invisible to an outside observer.

A participant who managed their shopping independently last week may be unable to leave their home this week. That is not inconsistency. That is their disability.

On TMG180, fluctuation is documented as information — not as a deviation from a baseline. The monthly snapshot includes a non-linear functioning statement in every single report. This statement exists to protect participants at reassessment. A harder month does not mean they need less support. Often it means they need more.


The intake form as a whole-person baseline

When a participant joins TMG180 they complete a Functional Capacity Assessment intake. This is not a clinical assessment. It is a whole-person conversation.

It asks about their personality, what gives their life meaning, what their strengths are, how their disability affects their daily life across six functional areas, what support patterns look like over time, and what relational compatibility matters to them.

This intake becomes the baseline. Everything you observe in your daily notes is compared against it. Everything in the monthly snapshot builds from it. The intake is not a one-time form — it is a living reference point for the entire support relationship.

When you read a participant's intake before your first session, take it seriously. It took courage to write. It reflects what they want you to know about them before you meet.


What participants often cannot do — and why that matters

Many participants on this platform have spent years trying to fit themselves into systems that poorly reflected their reality. They have learned to minimise, to manage expectations, to perform capability they do not always have.

Some will downplay their needs in sessions with you. Some will overestimate what they can manage independently. Some will cancel supports during the periods they need them most — because reaching out when overwhelmed feels impossible.

Your job is to document what you actually observe — not what the participant says they can do. If a participant tells you they are fine but presents as exhausted and withdrawn, both things can be true and both things belong in your note.

MODULE 4
Documentation on TMG180
5 minute read  •  Practical skills  •  TMG180 Learning Hub
	| 


	| Notes on this platform are not about proving you were there. They are about building a picture of a person's life over time — one that they own, that they can use, and that reflects their actual experience.
	| 

The two-layer model

Every note you write on TMG180 has two layers. Understanding the difference between them is the most important thing to understand about documentation on this platform.

Layer 1 — Your private narrative notes
These are completely private. Only you can see them. They are stored separately from the participant record and are only accessible in legal proceedings. You write honestly here. You process your observations, your concerns, your professional judgements. This is your space.

Layer 2 — The structured support event log
This is what flows into the participant's record. It takes 30 to 60 seconds. It uses checkboxes — not long narratives. It links to goals from the participant's intake, captures functional domain tags, describes what support was delivered and what it made possible, and notes how today compared to the participant's baseline.

The participant can see everything in Layer 2. They can add their own context. They approve the monthly summary built from it. It belongs to them.


What the structured event log captures


	| Goal links — which of the participant's NDIS goals did today's support connect to? (1-3, mandatory)
Functional domain tags — which NDIS functional areas were involved? (1-3, mandatory)
What was hard today — functional impacts observed, not diagnosed
Support delivered — what type of support did you provide?
Outcome micro-block — what did the support make possible today? Small wins count.
Baseline comparison — was today typical, harder, or different from the participant's usual pattern?
Participant voice — anything the participant said or indicated that is worth recording
Safety — any concerns, or confirmation that there are none
	| 
You cannot finalise a note without selecting goals and functional domain tags. This is not a restriction. It is a protection — for the participant's evidence record and for your documentation integrity.


Language rules in documentation

These eight terms must never appear in any documentation on this platform:

Never write this
	| Write this instead
	| Why
	| match
	| filter / search
	| Matching implies algorithm control over participant choice
	| assigned caseload
	| participants you support (with consent)
	| Caseload is provider language. Consent is participant language.
	| tasks
	| support event logs / notes / invoices
	| Tasks frames support as productivity output
	| productivity
	| clarity / calm workspace
	| Productivity frames the worker as an output unit
	| programs
	| supports / snapshot
	| Programs implies institutional structure
	| clinical notes
	| support logs / snapshot notes
	| Clinical implies medical oversight
	| ACTION REQUIRED
	| Needs review
	| Urgency language creates anxiety, not action
	| non-compliant
	| not completed yet
	| Non-compliant is a judgement. Not completed yet is information.
	| 

What good notes look like


	| Not this:
"Assisted client with daily tasks. All good. No concerns."

This:
"Participant presented as fatigued today with reduced capacity to initiate tasks.
Support provided with meal preparation and a short walk. Participant required
step-by-step prompting throughout. With support, participant was able to complete
planned activities and reported feeling calmer by end of session.
This session supported the participant's goal to maintain daily living independence.
Capacity lower than baseline today — consistent with fluctuating support needs.
No safety concerns."

MODULE 5
Your Role, Your Sustainability, Your Autonomy
5 minute read  •  Worker wellbeing  •  TMG180 Learning Hub
	| 


	| You are not an employee of TMG180. You are not a subcontractor managed by a provider. You are an independent professional operating within a governed ecosystem that exists to support you — not direct you.
	| 

What TMG180 gives you

Most independent workers in the NDIS face a choice between two bad options. Work under a provider and lose your autonomy. Or work completely independently and carry all the compliance burden yourself.

TMG180 is the middle ground. You keep your ABN, your rates, your relationships with participants, and your professional identity. The platform provides the infrastructure you would otherwise have to build yourself.


	| Compliance tracking — document expiry alerts, screening verification, insurance monitoring
Governance framework — the practice standards and code of conduct you operate under
Documentation system — structured note templates that are legally defensible
Directory visibility — participants can find you based on your skills and relational fit
Learning Hub — ongoing professional development built around the relational framework
AI-assisted workflows — intake summaries, snapshot drafts, event log helpers
Participant consent architecture — clear, auditable records of who has access to what
	| 

What TMG180 does not do

TMG180 does not roster you. Does not set your rates. Does not tell you how to run a session. Does not take a percentage of your income. Does not manage your relationship with participants. Does not own your professional practice.

You are responsible for your own tax, superannuation, and invoicing. You are responsible for your own professional conduct and the quality of your support. You are responsible for maintaining your compliance documents.

The platform gives you the structure. You bring the practice.


Worker sustainability

Burnout in the NDIS independent workforce is real. Fear-based documentation, governance confusion, operational overwhelm, and working without support structures take a toll. This platform was partly built in response to that.

The documentation system is designed to take 30 to 60 seconds after a session — not 45 minutes. The relational framework is designed to give you language and structure that makes practice feel clear, not confusing. The governance documents are designed to help you understand what you are operating within, not to add to your anxiety.

If you find the platform adds to your administrative burden rather than reducing it, that is worth raising. The platform should make your work easier and more sustainable, not harder.


The relational framework applies to you too

Everything this platform asks of you in your relationship with participants, it also holds for your relationship with the platform itself.

You will not be shamed for a harder week. You will not be flagged as non-compliant for a document that has not been renewed yet — you will receive a needs review notice. You will not be judged for asking questions or raising concerns.

The same dignity-preserving, non-clinical, non-shaming framework that protects participants also protects workers on this platform. That is not an accident. It is intentional.


One final thing

This platform was built by someone who has lived the experience of needing support within an imperfect system. She built it for participants. But she also built it for workers like you — who do this work because it matters, who want to do it well, and who deserve infrastructure that actually supports that.

Welcome to TMG180.
