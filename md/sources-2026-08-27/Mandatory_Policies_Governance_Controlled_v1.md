	| 1. Executive Summary
TMG180 is a digital relational ecosystem platform. It is not a traditional NDIS provider, does not employ workers and does not deliver supports directly.
The mandatory policies define the governance conditions for safe participation in the platform. They clarify the platform’s role, worker independence, participant rights, privacy boundaries, platform-level complaint and incident pathways, risk expectations, cybersecurity safeguards, fair trading obligations and inclusive practice commitments.
The policies are designed to protect the human-centred intent of the NDIS while avoiding provider-style overreach. TMG180 provides infrastructure, templates, policy alignment, privacy-by-design workflows, verification tracking and platform-level governance events. It does not supervise or direct the day-to-day delivery of supports.
This governance-controlled manual makes the policy architecture auditable. It shows how each policy connects to a specific governance risk, legal foundation, platform boundary and evidence pathway.
CORE GOVERNANCE INTERPRETATION
TMG180’s mandatory policy framework is not an employment handbook and not a provider service-delivery manual. It is a platform participation framework that supports independent workers and participant-owned records while maintaining clear boundaries between platform governance and worker-controlled service delivery.
	| 2. Platform Boundary Statement
TMG180 is the software platform and wider digital relational ecosystem. The Worker Portal and Participant Portal are role-based areas within the same platform.
Governance Administration means platform governance metadata only, including verification status, policy versions and acknowledgements, platform-level incident or complaint tickets and platform access controls.
The Participant Portal is a participant-owned workspace. It may contain a Participant Personal Profile baseline, participant check-ins, structured support event logs, participant-approved monthly snapshot exports and consent controls, where the participant chooses to use the portal and provides consent.
The Worker Portal is the subscribed worker’s workspace. It may include templates, invoicing tools, structured logs, library resources, directory/profile controls and optional worker-controlled storage functions.
TMG180 does not roster, allocate, direct or supervise independent workers. It does not manage day-to-day supports, observe support sessions, make decisions for participants, replace professional judgement, or access worker-controlled narrative service records.
Independent workers remain self-employed practitioners, sole traders, subcontractors or independent professionals operating under their own legal, business, insurance, tax, scope-of-practice and safeguarding responsibilities.

Mandatory Policy 2: Complaints, Concerns and Feedback
GOVERNANCE CONTROL PURPOSE
Provide safe, non-retaliatory and proportionate pathways for feedback and complaints without turning every concern into punishment.
	| LEGAL AND REGULATORY BASIS
NDIS Code of Conduct; NDIS Act 2013 choice and control principles; Privacy Act 1988; consumer protection principles; digital platform safeguarding expectations.
	| Purpose of this Policy
The purpose of this policy is to explain how complaints, concerns, and feedback are received and
responded to within TMG180 Governance Administration in a way that is respectful,
transparent, trauma-informed, and consistent with legislative obligations.
This policy recognises that feedback and complaints are a normal part of working in complex
human systems It exists to provide safe pathways for raising concerns without fear, blame, or
retaliation, while ensuring serious issues are addressed appropriately.
TMG180 Governance Administration is part of a digital platform and not a NDIS service
provider This policy does not replace service delivery complaint processes between workers and
participants.
Scope
This policy applies to:
Participants using TMG180 180 digital platform
Independent workers operating through the platform
Platform administrators, in relation to governance and platform safety only
This policy does not apply to:
employment disputes
clinical supervision
provider level complaint handling

An expression of dissatisfaction, concern, or discomfort about conduct, safety, communication, or
platform use.
Feedback
Information provided with the intention of improving understanding, relationships, or systems.
Platform Related Complaint
A complaint about the operation of TMG180 180, including platform conduct, policies,
verification processes, or safety mechanisms.
Service Related Complaint
A complaint about support delivery, which is primarily managed between the participant and the
independent worker.

not all concerns require punitive action

conduct that feels inappropriate or uncomfortable

assess whether the issue is platform related or service related
respond proportionately
provide guidance or clarification

Mandatory Policy 3: Incident Management
GOVERNANCE CONTROL PURPOSE
Separate platform-level incident governance from worker-controlled service-level incident responsibilities while preserving escalation options.
	| LEGAL AND REGULATORY BASIS
NDIS Code of Conduct; NDIS Act 2013 dignity, choice and control principles; WHS principles; Privacy Act 1988; digital platform safeguarding expectations.
	| Purpose of this Policy
The purpose of this policy is to explain how incidents are recognised, responded to, documented,
and escalated by TMG180 Governance Administration in a way that supports safety,
transparency, and ethical practice for both participants and workers.
This policy acknowledges that incidents can arise in complex, human situations, even where
people are acting in good faith It exists to provide clear, supportive pathways for responding to
concerns without blame, fear, or unrealistic expectations.
TMG180 Governance Administration oversee a digital platform and is not a NDIS service
provider Independent workers remain responsible for service delivery and for managing incidents
that arise within their own practice.
Scope
This policy applies to:
Participants using TMG180 180 digital platform
Independent workers operating through the platform
Platform administrators, in relation to governance, safeguarding, and platform integrity
This policy does not apply to:
clinical incident review
provider level incident management
employment based disciplinary processes
Definitions
Incident
Any event, situation, or pattern of behaviour that raises concern about safety, wellbeing,
boundaries, or ethical practice This may include physical, emotional, relational, environmental, or
digital safety issues.
Platform Level Incident
An incident that relates to platform governance, misuse of the platform, worker eligibility, serious
boundary breaches, or conduct that may affect the safety or integrity of the platform.
Service Level Incident
An incident that occurs during support delivery and is primarily managed by the independent
worker as part of their professional responsibilities.
Legal and Regulatory Basis
This policy aligns with and is informed by:
NDIS Code of Conduct, including obligations to act safely and with integrity
NDIS Act 2013 principles of dignity, choice, and control
Work Health and Safety principles, including psychosocial safety
Privacy Act 1988
Digital platform safeguarding expectations
Principles Guiding Incident Management
TMG180 Governance Administration operates on the principle that:
safety includes physical, emotional, relational, and psychological wellbeing
incidents are not always the result of wrongdoing
early identification reduces harm
people are more likely to act ethically when they feel safe to speak
context matters
proportionate responses are essential
What this means at TMG180
This policy is designed to support learning, safety, and accountability, not to punish people for

Recognising Incidents

emotional distress or trauma activation
boundary crossings or role confusion
aggressive, threatening, or intimidating behaviour
unsafe or unstable environments
significant breakdowns in communication

Responsibilities in Relation to Incidents

Participants and workers are expected to:

prioritise safety for themselves and others
communicate concerns in a way that feels safe and manageable
seek support or clarification when unsure how to respond
engage in discussion where appropriate and safe to do so

What this means at TMG180
No one is expected to cope alone or to have all the answers in the moment.
Worker Responsibilities in Service Level Incidents
Independent workers remain responsible for:

pausing, adjusting, or leaving situations that feel unsafe

documenting incidents privately using WCPS or another secure system
reflecting on what occurred and adjusting practice where appropriate

Safety grows when people feel able to speak honestly, regardless of role.

The platform records only information necessary to manage platform safety and governance.
Response to Patterns or Ongoing Risk
Where patterns of concern emerge, TMG180 Governance Administration will:
seek clarification
provide guidance
recommend changes to platform participation
suspend or remove access to protect safety
Actions are proportionate and focused on prevention and integrity rather than punishment.

Mandatory Policy 4: Consent and Information Handling
GOVERNANCE CONTROL PURPOSE
Protect consent, participant autonomy, privacy and information minimisation across participant-owned portal records and worker-controlled records.
	| LEGAL AND REGULATORY BASIS
Privacy Act 1988 and APPs; NDIS Act 2013 choice and control principles; NDIS Code of Conduct; human rights principles; digital platform governance expectations.
	| Handling Policy
Purpose of this Policy
The purpose of this policy is to set out how consent is obtained, recorded, respected, and
withdrawn across TMG180 Governance Administration digital platform, and how
information is handled in a lawful, ethical, and human centered way.
Scope
This policy applies to:
Independent workers operating to TMG180 Governance Administration
Participants and their authorised representatives
Platform administrators in relation to governance and oversight functions
This policy does not apply to clinical governance, service delivery supervision, or provider level
record keeping, as these functions are not performed by TMG180 180.
Definitions
Consent
Freely given, informed, specific, and voluntary agreement to an action or decision, which can be
withdrawn at any time.
Information
Any personal, sensitive, or service related information relating to a participant or worker.
WCPS (Worker Controlled Private Storage)
A secure, encrypted storage environment within a worker’s dashboard that is accessible only by
the worker and not by TMG180 Governance Administration.
Legal and Regulatory Basis
This policy aligns with and is informed by:
Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and Australian Privacy Principles
NDIS Act 2013, including principles of choice and control
NDIS Code of Conduct, including respect for rights, informed decision-making, and privacy
Human rights principles relevant to disability and autonomy
Digital platform governance expectations
Principles of Consent
Consent under TMG180 Governance Administration must be:
Informed
V oluntary
Specific to the task or information
Ongoing
Able to be withdrawn without penalty
Workers are expected to ensure participants understand:
What information is being recorded or shared
Why it is being recorded or shared
How it will be used
Who will have access
Their right to say no or change their mind
What this means at TMG180
Consent should feel safe and respectful Workers are expected to use plain, human language and to
check in regularly, especially where capacity fluctuates or trauma is present.
Obtaining and Withdrawing Consent
Independent workers are responsible for:
Seeking consent before recording information
Rechecking consent when circumstances change
Respecting withdrawal of consent immediately
Adjusting documentation and practice accordingly
Consent must never be assumed, implied, or coerced.
Participants may withdraw consent:

communicate honestly and respectfully with the participant where appropriate
reschedule or organise alternative support where possible
This is not a failure of professionalism It is a preventative safety measure.
Participants are not responsible for regulating a worker’s emotional state, and workers are not

integrity.

Naming limits early protects everyone and reduces harm.

	| 6. Governance Risks and Controls
Risk
	| Control within this manual
	| TMG180 is mistaken for an employer or direct NDIS provider.
	| Repeated platform boundary statement, worker terms, participant/public statement and service-delivery separation.
	| Platform governance overreaches into worker-controlled service delivery.
	| WCPS separation, platform-level incident/complaint classification and role-based access controls.
	| Participant records become centralised or over-collected.
	| Participant-owned snapshot model, consent controls, data minimisation and privacy-by-design.
	| Independent workers operate without clarity or support.
	| Mandatory policy acknowledgement, templates, governance library, risk and incident pathways.
	| Complaints or feedback are treated punitively.
	| Trauma-informed complaints framework, anti-retaliation, proportionality and external escalation options.
	| AI creates privacy or decision-making risk.
	| Internal AI boundaries, draft-only outputs, no WCPS access, no public AI use with identifiable participant information.
	| Terms become unfair, hidden or restrictive.
	| Fair trading policy, fee transparency, no exclusivity, no percentage of worker earnings and opt-out rights.
